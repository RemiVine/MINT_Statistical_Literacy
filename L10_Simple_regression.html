Lecture 10 · Correlation & Univariate Linear Regression

Quiz: Education and Child Mortality

A development economist investigates the relationship between girls' secondary-school completion and under-five mortality across 100 countries.

Dependent variable (Y): Under-five mortality, measured as deaths per 1,000 live births.

Explanatory variable (X): Girls' secondary-school completion rate, measured in percent.

The estimated univariate OLS regression is:

Ŷ = 110 − 0.80X
Variable Coefficient Standard error p-value
Girls' secondary-school completion −0.80 0.15 < 0.001
Constant 110.00 8.00 < 0.001

Observations: 100 countries

R²: 0.64

Note: All regression results are hypothetical and intended for educational purposes.

Question 1 — Interpreting the slope

How should the coefficient −0.80 be interpreted?

Explanation: The slope measures the predicted change in Y for a one-unit increase in X. Here, one unit of X is one percentage point, and Y is measured in deaths per 1,000 live births. The relationship is associational, not necessarily causal.

Question 2 — Interpreting the intercept

What does the intercept of 110 represent?

Explanation: The intercept is the predicted value of Y when X = 0. Whether a zero completion rate is realistic or meaningful depends on the context and the observed data.

Question 3 — Making predictions

What is the predicted under-five mortality rate for a country where girls' secondary-school completion is 75%?

Explanation: Substitute X = 75 into the estimated equation: Ŷ = 110 − 0.80 × 75 = 50 deaths per 1,000 live births.

Question 4 — Understanding residuals

A country has a completion rate of 50% and an observed under-five mortality rate of 80 deaths per 1,000 live births. What is its residual?

Explanation: The predicted mortality rate is Ŷ = 110 − 0.80 × 50 = 70. The residual is observed minus predicted: e = 80 − 70 = +10 deaths per 1,000 live births.

Question 5 — Statistical inference

The slope has a p-value below 0.001. What can we conclude at the 5% significance level, when testing H₀: β₁ = 0 against H₁: β₁ ≠ 0?

Explanation: Since p < 0.001 < 0.05, we reject H₀. There is evidence of a statistically significant linear association. Statistical significance does not establish causality, and the p-value is not the probability that H₀ is true.

Question 6 — Interpreting R²

What does R² = 0.64 tell us about the regression?

Explanation: R² measures the proportion of variation in Y accounted for by the fitted regression. Here, the model accounts for 64% of the sample variation in under-five mortality. In simple OLS with an intercept, the correlation is −√0.64 = −0.80.
